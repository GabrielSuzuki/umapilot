web: let the player set the run, instead of assuming one captured career

Until now `defaultSetup()` loaded `examples/real-run.json` unconditionally, and
the app exposed no way to change it. So every recommendation it has ever given
was computed against one particular 2026-09-05 deck, whatever the player was
actually playing.

That is not a small approximation. Which facilities a deck can rainbow is the
largest single lever on what gets recommended -- `deck-and-rainbow.md` is
entirely about it -- so a wrong deck is a wrong premise under a correct
calculation, and the correctness of the calculation makes it harder to notice
rather than easier. The reader can be perfect about the turn screen and the
advice still be about someone else's run.

A third tab: the deck, the caps and the starting line.

CARDS ARE PICKED, NOT TYPED. Hand-entering friendship bonuses and mood effects
would be tedious and wrong, and the real values are already in the dataset
extracted from the player's own master.mdb -- 250 cards with their effects
tabled by level. Picking by name also makes the scenario-link count correct for
free, because the engine derives it from card IDs against
`dataset.scenarioLinkedCards`: a real ID is a real link.

`effectAt` resolves a level table without interpolating, because the game does
not: an effect holds its tabled value until the next tabled level.

The scenario is REBUILT on a setup change rather than patched -- changing a card
changes what the engine is -- but the editable turn state is kept. A new deck
does not mean a new turn, new stats or a new target, and resetting them would
make the pane unusable: entering six cards would wipe the state six times.

Saved to localStorage, because a deck is entered once per career and asking
again on every reload would guarantee it goes stale instead. Reads and writes
are wrapped: storage throws in a private window and comes back empty after site
data is cleared, and a setup pane that cannot render is worse than one that
starts from the default.

An empty or half-entered deck falls back to the captured career rather than
constructing a scenario with no cards, so the app stays usable while the player
is still choosing.

What this does NOT do is read the deck off the screen. That is M3d and
`docs/scan-spec.md` describes it, including why card identity is the hard part
(235 of them, full-bleed art) and why it always ends in a confirmation step. The
confirmation step is this pane. Hand entry first, automation onto it later.
