objective: name the 1200 halving, and say it out loud

Grand Concert raises the stat caps past 1200 and then halves everything
above that line for race mechanics: 1600 Speed races as 1400. Nothing in
the engine knew that. `shortfallScore` priced a point at 1500 exactly
like a point at 500, and OVERSHOOT_WEIGHT paid full rate for going past
a target, deep into the halved zone.

It is NOT a training penalty. You gain the full points at the full rate;
they are worth half when raced. `computeTraining` is right as it stands
and is untouched -- the correction belongs in what a build is WORTH, not
in what a training yields.

Three changes:

1. `effectiveStat(raw)` and `HALVING_THRESHOLD` in objective.ts, with
   the reasoning and the provenance attached. The rule is
   community-sourced (uma.guide's Grand Concert page), not decoded from
   master.mdb, so it is flagged as such.

2. `CompiledTarget.raceEffective`, plumbed through `plan()` as an
   option. OFF BY DEFAULT, deliberately: a player who asks for a
   displayed 1600 -- for the number, or for what it passes down as a
   spark -- wants raw, and quietly rescoring their goal would be the
   same class of error as silently optimising around an unreachable
   target. It is a switch, not a correction.

3. `validateTarget` now warns when a stat target sits above 1200,
   naming what it races as. This is where it actually matters: the
   warning fires before 72 turns are spent, which is what that function
   exists for.

Measured, so the size is on record rather than assumed: with the flag on
and a 1600-Speed target the search shifts its training mix (sp13/wi26 ->
sp14/wi19) and ends 93 points higher. But no simulated career currently
reaches 1200 in ANY stat -- the model tops out near 974 Speed -- so today
this changes the advice at the margin and nothing else. It starts to
matter when the simulator can produce builds like a real career's 1635
Speed, which needs races, events, summer camp and songs first.

The smoke test asserted `validateTarget(...).length === 0` for a legality
question. That conflated "no problems" with "no errors" and broke on the
first legitimate warning. It now filters by severity, which is what it
meant, plus two checks pinning the new warning and its absence at 1200.
