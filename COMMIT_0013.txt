planner: stop the search branching on turns that cannot help

The energy price was not inflated. Measured against 200-rollout ground
truth it averages ~0.0002 objective units per point, which is the figure
"100 energy buys four or five trainings" implies. What it is instead is
NOISY: at 12 samples the standard error is about the size of the price,
so the estimate's sign is close to a coin flip -- across nine states of
one career it came back negative on four. `resourceValue` multiplies that
by an energy stock of 20-80, which put a +/-0.03 term on interior scores
whose real differences are about +/-0.007.

Chasing the price downward would have been chasing half of a symmetric
distribution. Three changes, in the order they were found:

1. Rollouts address their randomness by turn, not by stream position.
   Two rollouts under one sequential generator stay in step only until
   the first turn where they act differently -- a training draws a
   failure roll and a token roll, a rest draws one number -- so one
   divergence at turn 3 shifts every draw after it. That is the same
   defect the leaf estimator was patched around with leafSamples: 8,
   fixed at its source: `rollout` now takes a seed and derives a
   generator per (turn, purpose). Truncated and full-career prices now
   agree, where before they disagreed by 3x.

2. Prices carry an error bar and are weighted by it. Each price is
   shrunk toward zero by its own signal-to-noise, w = m^2/(m^2+se^2),
   and reports `stderr`, `raw` and the weight applied. No tuning
   constant: w is computed from the samples. This project's rule is that
   a sampled quantity is never rendered without an interval, and the
   shadow prices were the one exemption.

3. The search no longer branches on actions the model itself makes
   pointless. Rest at 100 energy clamps to no gain; the infirmary is
   +10 energy in a model with no condition to clear, which rest strictly
   beats on every turn. Both stay legal -- the scenario models what the
   player may do -- but `turnCandidates` drops them, and plan() says so
   in its assumptions rather than omitting them silently.

The third is what mattered. Measured over five seeds at cheap settings:

  trainings   40-44  ->  53-57   (placement-greedy takes 51)
  wasted      2-7    ->  0
  income      698-786 -> 872-958
  stat sum    2102   ->  2500 mean, every seed improved

The first two are worth keeping on their own terms -- the estimator is
now consistent and says how much to trust it -- but neither moved a
career much, and saying so is the point: the damage was the search
spending turns on moves that could not help, not the price it put on
energy.

Still open, and now measurable: plan() reports its top two actions as
inseparable on ~69% of turns at these settings, and the diagnostic takes
recommendations[0] regardless. That is a sample-count problem.

Tests pin the bug as well as the fix: every filter check is paired with
one asserting the scenario still offers the action, so the fixture cannot
quietly lose the ability to express the failure.

.gitignore picks up the local scratch that has been sitting untracked in
the repo root -- the diagnose-* harnesses, the probe-* scripts, and the
delivered patch files. Untracked files count as a dirty tree, so
apply-patches.ps1 was refusing to run while they were there.
